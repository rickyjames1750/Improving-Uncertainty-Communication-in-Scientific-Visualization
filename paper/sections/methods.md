# 3. Methods

## 3.1 Participants

We recruited 16 participants through convenience sampling, friends and
classmates from a range of departments and universities. All had normal or corrected-to-normal
vision and were comfortable reading English. The sample skewed young (8 aged
18–24, 6 aged 25–34, one 35–44, one 45+) and technically literate
(self-rated data literacy: 3 participants at level 3, 4 at level 4, 9 at
level 5; median = 5). Fields of study included Computer Science (4),
Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Data Science, Bioengineering,
Physics, Mathematics, Psychology, Symbolic Systems, Business Administration,
Interaction Design, Computational Health, and Chicanx/Latinx Studies (1 each).
Participation was voluntary and anonymous; no compensation was offered.

## 3.2 Materials

### Stimuli

Three chart types were generated from the Palmer Penguins dataset (Horst
et al., 2020), each showing bill length (mm) by species (Adelie, Chinstrap,
Gentoo):

1. **Error bars:** bar chart of species means with 95% confidence interval
   error bars. This is the baseline condition, representing the most common
   way uncertainty is shown in published figures.
2. **Violin + box plot:** violin plot overlaid with a box plot, showing the
   full distribution shape plus median and quartiles.
3. **HOPs grid:** a 3×4 grid of 12 bootstrap draws. Each panel shows one
   resampled set of species means as dot/lollipop plots. An italic caption
   at the bottom reads "Each panel shows one plausible outcome from
   resampling the data."

All stimuli were generated by `stimuli/generate_stimuli.py` using matplotlib
3.9.4 and are available in the repository under `stimuli/figures/`.

### Tasks

To prevent answer carry-over between encoding blocks, we used three parallel
task sets with structurally identical question types but different content:

- **Set 1 (Block 1):** Ranking: which species has the *largest* mean bill
  length (Chinstrap). Estimation: estimate the mean for Chinstrap (48.8 mm).
  Decision: claim that Gentoo and Chinstrap have the same mean (Disagree).
- **Set 2 (Block 2):** Ranking: which species has the *smallest* mean
  (Adelie). Estimation: estimate the mean for Gentoo (47.5 mm). Decision:
  claim that Adelie and Gentoo have the same mean (Disagree).
- **Set 3 (Block 3):** Ranking: which species has the *second-largest* mean
  (Gentoo). Estimation: estimate the mean for Adelie (38.8 mm). Decision:
  claim that Chinstrap has a larger mean than Gentoo (Agree).

Estimation answers were scored correct if within ±2 mm of the true mean.
After each task, participants rated their confidence on a 1–5 Likert scale
(1 = not at all confident, 5 = very confident).

### Platform

A self-contained HTML/CSS/JS page (`study/survey.html`). Stimuli were static
PNG images embedded in the page. Response timing was recorded using
`performance.now()` from stimulus onset to response submission. No server
was required; results were displayed as JSON for manual collection via
copy-to-clipboard and download buttons.

## 3.3 Design

Within-subjects: every participant saw all three encoding conditions. Encoding
order was counterbalanced by randomly shuffling the three conditions per
session, giving each participant a unique permutation drawn uniformly from the
six possible orderings. Task order within each encoding block was fixed
(ranking → estimation → decision).

A brief arithmetic distractor task appeared between each encoding block to
break short-term retention and reduce carry-over effects. At the end of the
session, participants answered a recognition-check question ("Did you feel
you remembered answers from earlier charts?") to provide self-report data
on whether carry-over was still present.

This yields 9 trials per participant (3 encodings × 3 tasks), for a total
of 144 trial observations across the 16 participants.

## 3.4 Procedure

1. Consent page with study description and time estimate (~10 minutes).
2. Demographics: age range, major/field, self-rated data literacy (1–5).
3. Instructions: brief explanation of what participants would see and do.
4. Nine trials in three blocks (one per encoding). Each trial presented a
   chart, a question, and a confidence rating. A short arithmetic distractor
   appeared between blocks.
5. Recognition check: "Did you feel you remembered answers from earlier
   charts?"
6. Open-ended feedback: "Any comments or observations about the charts?"
7. Thank-you page displaying collected data in JSON format with options to
   copy or download.

Most participants were walked through the survey over FaceTime or Zoom; a
few used the hosted link independently.

## 3.5 Measures

- **Decision accuracy:** proportion of correct responses per encoding (binary
  per trial).
- **Confidence calibration:** Brier score, (confidence/5 − correct)²,
  averaged per condition. Lower is better. A Brier score of 0 means perfect
  calibration.
- **Response time:** milliseconds from stimulus onset to response submission,
  converted to seconds for reporting.

## 3.6 Analysis Plan

- Descriptive statistics: per-encoding accuracy, mean confidence, mean
  response time, Brier score.
- Friedman test (non-parametric repeated-measures) for accuracy and response
  time across the three encodings, since the data may not meet normality
  assumptions with this sample size.
- Post-hoc pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests if the Friedman test is
  significant.
- Per-task-type breakdowns to check whether effects are driven by a specific
  task.
- Block-position analysis to check for order effects, even with the parallel
  questions and distractors.
- All analysis code is in `analysis/analyze_responses.py`.
